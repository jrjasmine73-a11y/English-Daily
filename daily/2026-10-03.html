1. Why does heartbreak hurt so much?

BBC Learning English · 6 Minute English · 2026-10-03 · 原文链接
📖 本期 BBC《六分钟英语》探讨了一个人人都会好奇的问题：为什么心碎会痛得像真的一样？节目从脑科学出发揭示：心碎时大脑中负责痛感的区域真的会被点亮；热恋则会让大脑对伴侣"上瘾"，甚至重塑我们的自我认知。节目还聊到 1950 年代的依恋理论，以及牢固的社交纽带为什么是长寿的首要预测指标，语言难度适中，值得细读。
¶ 1

NeilHi Georgie.

尼尔：乔治，你好。

¶ 2

GeorgieHi Neil.

乔治：尼尔，你好。

¶ 3

NeilNow, I want you to think back to the last time you had your heart broken. How did it feel?

尼尔：现在，请回想一下你上一次心碎的经历。那是什么感觉？

¶ 4

GeorgieIt was a couple of years ago. And I just remember a really intense, sad emotion. But it also sometimes feels like a physical pain in your stomach, doesn't it?

乔治：那是几年前的事情了。我只记得一种非常强烈的悲伤情绪。但有时候还会觉得肚子里真的像在疼，你也有过吧？

¶ 5

NeilIt does. It's like real pain, isn't it? And today, in 6 Minute English, we're talking about heartbreak. Now, heartbreak is the feeling of sadness we get when we lose someone we love. And that pain you feel, it's not just imaginary. In this podcast, we'll be understanding how our brains change when we fall in love and asking the question, why does heartbreak hurt so much?

尼尔：有过。那就像真实的疼痛，对吧？今天在《六分钟英语》里，我们来聊聊心碎。心碎是指失去所爱之人时感受到的悲伤。而你体会到的那种痛，并不是想象出来的。这一期节目中，我们将了解坠入爱河时大脑会发生什么变化，并探讨一个问题：为什么心碎会这么痛？

be oriented / orientated towards 短语 以……为重心；朝向……e.g. You're oriented completely toward the other person. It's like hunger or thirst.（你完全以对方为重心，就像饥饿或口渴一样。）
¶ 6

GeorgieAnd as usual, we'll be learning some useful new vocabulary. You can find the full list and the definitions at bbclearningenglish.com.

乔治：和往常一样，我们还会学一些有用的新词汇。完整的词汇表和释义可以在 bbclearningenglish.com 上找到。

¶ 7

NeilGeorgie, we're going to start with a quiz question. Are you ready?

尼尔：乔治，我们先从一个小测验开始。准备好了吗？

¶ 8

GeorgieI am very ready.

乔治：我非常准备好了。

¶ 9

NeilSo, in the 1950s, psychologists noticed a link between children's early bonds with their parents and their romantic relationships as adults, but what is this theory called? Is it: a)    contraction theory,

尼尔：20 世纪 50 年代，心理学家发现儿童早期与父母的亲密关系和成年后的恋爱关系之间存在关联，但这个理论叫什么名字呢？是：a）收缩理论，

¶ 10

b)    attachment theory, orc)    superstition theory?

b）依恋理论，还是 c）迷信理论？

¶ 11

GeorgieI think it's b) attachment theory.

乔治：我觉得是 b）依恋理论。

¶ 12

NeilOK. Well, we will find out at the end of the programme.

尼尔：好。节目最后我们会揭晓答案。

¶ 13

GeorgieWhat's going on in our brains when we fall in love?

乔治：当我们坠入爱河时，大脑里到底发生了什么？

¶ 14

NeilWell, scientists have scanned people's brains while they're looking at a photo of their romantic partner. And they found that looking at someone you're in love with activates a part of the brain that also reminds us to eat and drink.

尼尔：科学家扫描了人们在看伴侣照片时的大脑。结果发现，看到心爱的人会激活大脑中也负责提醒我们进食和饮水的那个区域。

¶ 15

GeorgieAnd do scientists know why being in love impacts our brain in this way?

乔治：那科学家知道恋爱为什么会对大脑产生这样的影响吗？

¶ 16

NeilSo, it's all about survival. Our brains have evolved to be this way. Dr Lucy Brown explains more to the BBC World Service.

尼尔：这一切都与生存有关。我们的大脑就是进化成了这个样子。露西·布朗博士在 BBC 国际台上有进一步的解释。

¶ 17

Dr Lucy BrownThis is a survival system. Nature gave us these systems to be addicted to each other – for reproduction, for protection. You're oriented completely toward the other person. It's like hunger or thirst.

露西·布朗博士：这是一个生存系统。大自然给了我们这些系统，让我们对彼此"上瘾"——为了繁衍，也为了保护。你会完全以对方为重心，就像饥饿或口渴一样。

be addicted to 短语 对……上瘾；沉迷于……e.g. Nature gave us these systems to be addicted to each other — for reproduction, for protection.（大自然给了我们这些系统，让我们对彼此上瘾——为了繁衍，也为了保护。）
¶ 18

GeorgieLucy says our brains make us addicted to our romantic partners. If you're addicted to something, you want more of it. You can literally be addicted to things like drugs or alcohol, but we also use it metaphorically to talk about things we love.

乔治：露西说，我们的大脑让我们对恋人上瘾。如果你对某事上瘾，你就会不断想要更多。你可以真正对毒品或酒精上瘾，但我们也会用这个词来比喻性地谈论我们喜欢的东西。

¶ 19

NeilShe says that romantic partners are oriented to each other, which in British English we say orientated, and it means they are pointed towards each other. They think of each other before other people.

尼尔：她说恋人彼此"oriented"（英式英语说 orientated），意思是双方都朝向对方。在他们心里，对方永远排在别人前面。

¶ 20

GeorgieNeil, is there anything else scientists have discovered from scanning the brains of people in love?

乔治：尼尔，科学家从扫描热恋中人的大脑还发现了什么吗？

¶ 21

NeilWell, being in love doesn't just change the way we think. It changes our whole identity. A 2013 study showed us more about this. People were shown statements about themselves – for example, Neil is kind – and the part of the brain we use to think about ourselves lit up. When showing the same statements about their partner, the same part of the brain was active – not the part of the brain we generally use to think about others.

尼尔：恋爱不只改变我们的思维方式，它还改变了我们的整个自我认知。2013 年的一项研究对此有更多揭示。人们看到关于自己的陈述——比如"尼尔是个善良的人"——时，负责思考自我的大脑区域会被激活。而当看到关于伴侣的相同陈述时，活跃的竟然也是这个区域——而不是我们通常用来思考他人时的那个区域。

¶ 22

GeorgieSo, Neil, are all of these brain changes that come with being in love actually good for us?

乔治：那么，尼尔，恋爱带来的这些大脑变化，对我们都是好事吗？

¶ 23

NeilWell, studies suggest that good social connections help manage stress and support our immune system. Professor Zoe Donaldson explained more to the BBC World Service.

尼尔：研究表明，良好的社交关系有助于管理压力，也能支持我们的免疫系统。佐伊·唐纳森教授在 BBC 国际台上对此有更多解释。

¶ 24

Professor Zoe DonaldsonHaving really strong social bonds increases your likelihood that you'll survive from just about anything, whether that be cancer, depression, etc. And it overall is the number one predictor of longevity. So, if you are lonely and you don't have bonds, then your mortality risk goes up in a way that's equivalent to being obese or smoking.

佐伊·唐纳森教授：拥有真正牢固的社交纽带，会提高你战胜几乎任何疾病的几率，无论是癌症、抑郁症等等。而且它总体上是长寿的首要预测指标。所以，如果你孤独且没有这种纽带，你的死亡风险会上升，其程度相当于肥胖或吸烟。

social bonds 短语 社交纽带e.g. Having really strong social bonds increases your likelihood that you'll survive from just about anything.（拥有真正牢固的社交纽带，会提高你战胜几乎任何疾病的几率。）
longevity 短语 长寿；寿命e.g. It overall is the number one predictor of longevity.（它总体上是长寿的首要预测指标。）
¶ 25

GeorgieSo, having strong social bonds or close relationships with other people makes you more likely to survive disease, according to Zoe.

乔治：所以，根据佐伊的说法，拥有牢固的社交纽带、与他人保持亲密关系，能让人更有可能战胜疾病。

¶ 26

NeilYes, it's an important factor in our longevity, which means how long we live. In fact, Zoe says, being lonely and not having good social bonds increases your mortality risk, your risk of dying, by as much as being obese or smoking.

尼尔：是的，这是影响长寿（也就是寿命长短）的重要因素。事实上，佐伊说，孤独、缺乏良好社交纽带，会让你的死亡风险上升，其程度相当于肥胖或吸烟。

¶ 27

GeorgieOK. So, what happens when these bonds are broken, when we experience heartbreak?

乔治：好。那么，当这些纽带断裂、当我们经历心碎时，会发生什么呢？

¶ 28

NeilWell, scientists have been looking at this by asking people to look at photos of their exes, their former partners, and scanning their brains. They found that heartbreak activates parts of the brain we use when we're in danger or in pain. Professor Zoe Donaldson explains more.

尼尔：科学家通过让人们看前任的照片并扫描大脑来研究这个问题。他们发现，心碎会激活我们在感到危险或疼痛时使用的大脑区域。佐伊·唐纳森教授对此有更多解释。

¶ 29

Professor Zoe DonaldsonIt's literal pain in your brain. So, if you look at the parts of the brain that light up, they overlap with the same brain regions that are activated when you feel visceral pain – when you feel actual, you know... like you get a burn, or you break a... you break a bone.

佐伊·唐纳森教授：这是大脑层面真实的痛。如果你看哪些脑区被点亮，它们与你感受到内脏疼痛时——当你感受到真实的疼痛时，比如被烫伤、骨折——激活的脑区是重叠的。

light up 短语 （大脑区域）被点亮、激活e.g. If you look at the parts of the brain that light up, they overlap with the same brain regions that are activated when you feel visceral pain.（如果你看哪些脑区被点亮，它们与你感受到内脏疼痛时激活的脑区是重叠的。）
¶ 30

NeilOK. So, now it's time for the answer to the quiz question I asked you. It was about a theory developed by psychologists in the 1950s, which says that children's early relationships with their parents impact their relationships as adults. What's the name of the theory?

尼尔：好，现在来揭晓我之前问的小测验的答案。这个理论是 20 世纪 50 年代的心理学家提出的，说儿童早期与父母的关系会影响成年后的恋爱关系。这个理论叫什么名字？

¶ 31

GeorgieI was quite confident about this, wasn't I, Neil? I said attachment theory.

乔治：尼尔，我当时可是相当有信心，对吧？我说的是依恋理论。

¶ 32

NeilWell, you had a reason to be confident because you are correct.

尼尔：你有信心的理由，因为你说对了。

GeorgieYay! OK. Let's recap the vocabulary we learnt in this programme, starting with heartbreak, which is the feeling you experience after you lose someone you love.

乔治：太棒了！好，我们来回顾一下这期节目学到的词汇。首先是 heartbreak（心碎），指失去所爱之人后感受到的情绪。

¶ 34

NeilIf you're addicted to someone or something, you want more of it all the time.

尼尔：如果你对某人或某事 addicted（上瘾），你就总想不断地得到更多。

乔治：oriented（或 orientated）towards 是比喻用法，表示某人或某事是你的主要焦点。

尼尔：social bonds（社交纽带）指我们与家人、朋友和恋人之间的亲密关系。

乔治：longevity 指的是你能活多久。
